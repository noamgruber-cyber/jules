Common Chart Patterns

Learn to identify visual patterns that signal potential trend reversals or continuations.

What are Chart Patterns?

Chart patterns are recognizable shapes that emerge from price charts. Traders use these patterns to anticipate future price movements based on historical performance. They are broadly categorized into reversal patterns and continuation patterns.

Head and Shoulders

A Head and Shoulders is a reversal pattern consisting of three peaks: a higher peak (head) between two lower peaks (shoulders). It typically forms at the end of an uptrend and signals a potential bearish reversal.

Inverse Head and Shoulders is the bullish counterpart, forming at the end of a downtrend.

Double Top and Double Bottom

A Double Top looks like the letter "M". It occurs when the price reaches a high twice but fails to break through it, signaling a bearish reversal.

A Double Bottom looks like a "W". It forms when the price hits a low twice but fails to drop further, signaling a bullish reversal.

Triangles

Triangles are continuation patterns that show price consolidating before breaking out. There are three main types:

  • Ascending Triangle: Bullish pattern characterized by a flat upper trendline and a rising lower trendline.
  • Descending Triangle: Bearish pattern with a flat lower trendline and a falling upper trendline.
  • Symmetrical Triangle: Neutral pattern where both trendlines converge, indicating a breakout is imminent in either direction.
