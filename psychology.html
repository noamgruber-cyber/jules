Trading Psychology

Mastering your mind is just as important as mastering the charts.

The Emotional Pendulum

Trading is inherently emotional. Financial gains trigger euphoria, while losses trigger fear and anxiety. Successful traders learn to decouple their emotional state from their trading results, focusing entirely on the execution of their system.

FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out)

The anxiety that an exciting event (like a massive stock breakout) is currently happening elsewhere. FOMO causes traders to abandon their discipline, chase trades at terrible entry points, and completely ignore risk management.

Revenge Trading

The emotional urge to immediately win back money lost in a previous trade. This leads to forcing trades that don't meet your criteria, usually resulting in even larger losses. The market does not know or care that you just lost money.

Building Discipline

Discipline is doing what you are supposed to do, when you are supposed to do it, regardless of how you feel.

The Trading Journal

The most powerful psychological tool is a journal. Documenting your setup, entry logic, emotions during the trade, and the result forces you to be accountable to yourself and helps identify repetitive behavioral mistakes.

Accepting the Unknown

Mark Douglas, author of "Trading in the Zone," emphasizes that every moment in the market is unique. You must accept that you cannot know the outcome of any single trade. Your edge is derived from a large sample size of trades, not a single instance.
